What this app doesn't do (so there are no surprises)

It doesn't block devices for you. It identifies exactly which device is which —manufacturer, IP and MAC— so you can block it in your router settings, which is where that can be done. It also doesn't log into or change anything on your router: it only reads what it needs to show you the network.

It's an app for Windows PCs (not phones), and your PC has to be on and connected to the network to monitor it.

Questions that always come up

Do I have to set anything up on my router?

No. It installs like any Microsoft Store app and works with the WiFi you're already connected to.

Does it work on my phone?

No. It's a desktop app for Windows 10 and 11. There are phone apps for the same thing, but this one was designed for the PC: that's where you see the full list and can compare it with your router's page.

Can it tell who the person is, not just the device?

No, and be wary of any app that promises that. Devices are identified by their manufacturer and network address, not by their owner's name. What does work: turn devices off one by one until the unknown one disappears from the list, and that confirms which one it is.

How much does it cost?

The app is free: your full network, devices with their manufacturer, the speed test and the video diagnosis, with no time limit. If you also want the notices that tell you when something changes —Intruders, 7-day History and Alerts— they unlock with a one-time payment of USD 1.99 from inside the app.

Does it use a lot of PC resources?

No. The device scan is lightweight. It doesn't break data usage down per device: what it keeps is your connection's traffic. Desktop notifications can be turned off.

What if my PC is old or missing a Windows component?

It works anyway. The window is drawn by a Microsoft component (WebView2); if your computer doesn't have it —common on older or freshly installed Windows—, the app doesn't go blank: it tells you and opens by itself in your browser, with the same features. Install the component later and it goes back to its own window.
